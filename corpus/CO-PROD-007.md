# Engineering Handbook: Working Agreements

*Part 1 of the Engineering Handbook. Part 2 covers organizations and roles.*

When roughly 900 engineers work remote-first, habits matter more than meetings. This page collects the working agreements most teams share. Teams add their own on top, but start here.

## Write it down

If a decision affects other people, put it in writing: a Confluence page, a Jira ticket, or a pull request description. Chat is for conversation, not for records. When you decide something in a call, post the outcome afterward, with names, so that someone joining next quarter can find it.

## Planning cadence

- Teams plan in quarterly cycles and refine in two-week sprints.
- Each sprint ends with a short written update on what shipped and what did not.
- Technical Program Management supports planning for work that spans teams.

## Communication norms

- **Default to async.** Assume the reader is in a different time zone and may reply tomorrow.
- **Use threads.** Keep Slack channels readable by replying in threads.
- **Name an owner.** Every action item has one person's name next to it.
- **Mark urgency honestly.** Urgent means someone should drop what they are doing. Most things are not.

## Meetings

Meetings need an agenda and a note-taker. If there is no agenda a day before, cancel it or turn it into a document. Rotating the note-taker role spreads the load.

## Documentation habits

Every service should have a README that answers: what is this, who owns it, how do I run it, and where do I ask for help. Technical Writing publishes templates; use them, and ask for a review when a document will be read by people outside your team.

## Learning and growth

Engineers are encouraged to spend time on learning, internal talks, and mentoring. Managers should ask about growth in every one-on-one, not only at review time.

## Looking after each other

Take your time off. Hand work over clearly when you leave for a break. If you see a colleague stretched thin, say something, to them or to their manager.
