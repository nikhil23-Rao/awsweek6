# ADR Review Process FAQ

Answers to the questions the Architecture Review Board hears most often. For the policy itself, see the ADR Policy.

**Who reviews ADRs?**
The Architecture Review Board. The CTO chairs it, and members come from across the three engineering orgs.

**How do I submit one?**
Write the ADR using the template in the architecture Confluence space, set its status to Proposed, and post the link in the Board's Slack channel. A Board member will acknowledge it and tell you which review session to expect.

**How long does review take?**
It depends on how many teams are involved and how complete the draft is. Drafts that list affected teams and name a contact for each tend to move faster.

**Do I need approval from the teams I name?**
You should talk to them before you submit. The Board will usually ask whether affected teams have seen the draft, so having their comments in the document saves a round trip.

**What does the Board look for?**
A clear decision statement, real alternatives, honest consequences, and a list of who is affected. Length is not a goal; a one-page ADR is fine.

**Can I change an ADR after it is accepted?**
Small corrections such as typos and links are fine. If the decision itself changes, write a new ADR and link it to the old one so the history stays intact.

**What if I am not sure whether my change needs an ADR?**
Read the ADR Policy. If it is still unclear, ask in the Board's Slack channel.

**Where do I find existing ADRs?**
In the architecture records space in Confluence. Search by keyword or by team name.

**Who do I contact with other questions?**
Post in the Board's Slack channel, or ask your TPM.
