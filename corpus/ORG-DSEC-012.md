# DSEC Budget and Capacity Planning Cycle

This describes how DSEC plans money and people each year, so engineering managers know what is due and when.

## Annual calendar

| Month | Activity |
|---|---|
| September | Finance opens the planning cycle; DSEC managers review current spend |
| October | Teams submit headcount and vendor requests |
| November | Cloud spend forecasts due |
| December | VP reviews the combined plan with Finance, under the CFO |
| January | Budgets confirmed and communicated |
| Mid-year | Re-forecast based on actual spend |

## What goes into the plan

1. **Headcount:** new roles with a short justification tied to the roadmap.
2. **Cloud spend:** AWS costs per team, split into compute, storage, and data transfer. Forecasts should reflect known launches.
3. **Vendors and tools:** licenses, audit fees, and penetration testing engagements.
4. **Training and travel:** per-person allowances.

## Forecasting cloud cost

- Start from the last three months of actuals in the cost dashboards.
- Adjust for planned launches and decommissions.
- Add a contingency of about 10 percent.
- Explain any increase larger than 15 percent in words.

## Capacity planning

Engineering managers record each engineer's availability, leaving room for on-call, leave, and holidays. TPM combines these into the capacity sheet used in quarterly planning.

## Roles

- **Engineering managers:** prepare team inputs.
- **VP:** consolidates and negotiates.
- **TPM:** keeps the calendar and templates.
- **Finance partner:** reviews forecasts and flags anomalies.

## In-year changes

Spend increases of more than 10 percent of a team's budget during the year need VP approval and a note in the planning doc.
