# DSEC Stakeholder Communication Guide

Most trouble on projects starts with someone finding out too late. This guide sets habits for keeping stakeholders informed without burying them.

## Who your stakeholders are

Stakeholders are anyone who depends on your work, funds it, or is affected by it: other engineering orgs, Support, Legal, the CISO's office, Finance. List yours in the project page on day one.

## Status updates

Use the same three-part format every time:

1. **Where we are:** one sentence, with a color (green, yellow, red).
2. **What changed since last time.**
3. **What we need:** decisions, people, or dates.

Send updates on the cadence you promised. A short update on time beats a perfect one that is late.

## Colors mean something

- **Green:** on track; no help needed.
- **Yellow:** a risk exists; we have a plan; tell people early.
- **Red:** we will miss a commitment unless something changes; ask for help now.

Do not turn yellow into red at the last minute; people make better decisions with notice.

## Escalation etiquette

1. Raise it with the other team's tech lead first.
2. If unresolved after a week, bring in both engineering managers.
3. Take it to the VP only with a written summary of the options and what you recommend.

Escalations are about problems, not people. Write them so that they could be read aloud to the other party.

## During incidents

Follow the incident lead's instructions on channel and cadence. Do not start side threads asking for updates; read the main channel first.

## Meetings

Send an agenda in advance. Record decisions and actions in the notes within a day. If a meeting has no decision to make, consider an email.

## Tone

Be direct and kind. Say what you know, what you do not, and when you will know more.
